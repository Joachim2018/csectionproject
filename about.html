About the network

We exist to make cesarean recovery visible, supported, and hopeful.

We advocate for women whose birth stories deserve respect, care, and ongoing support.

Our story

MED C-Section Warriors Network began as a grassroots response to the gaps many women experience after cesarean birth. We noticed the need for a trusted community that could guide healing, share knowledge, and create pathways for advocacy.

Vision

A world where every mother who experiences a Caesarean birth is respected, supported, empowered, and celebrated throughout her journey of motherhood.

Mission

To improve the quality of life of mothers who undergo Caesarean births through advocacy, education, emotional wellness, healthcare partnerships, financial empowerment, research, and community support while promoting respectful maternal healthcare for all.

Values

Compassion, evidence-based care, inclusion, empowerment, and service.

Strategic objectives

  • Advance postpartum education and resources.
  • Support policy change for maternal care access.
  • Build a vibrant national and regional support network.

Our Inspiration - The founder’s story

MED C-SECTION WARRIORS NETWORK was born from lived experience—an experience that transformed pain into purpose. As our Founder, I have experienced two emergency Caesarean births. While each journey was different, both left lasting emotional scars that inspired me to establish this organization. During my first pregnancy, my husband and I eagerly awaited the birth of our first child. When labor began, I was admitted to the hospital ready for a normal delivery. Although I was due, my cervix would not dilate. I endured twelve hours of active labor before my doctor recommended induction in the hope that my body would respond. Even after two more hours of intense induction, there was still no progress. After fourteen hours of exhausting labor, my body began to shut down. I became extremely weak and suddenly lost the ability to breathe properly. I tried to call for help, but no words could come out. My mother noticed that I was struggling and immediately alerted the nurses. They rushed to my bedside, placed me on oxygen, and called the doctor. That was the moment my life was at risk. When the doctor arrived, he explained that I was no longer strong enough to continue labor. My baby was becoming distressed, and because I had already been placed on oxygen, attempting to continue pushing would endanger both my life and my baby’s life. The safest and only responsible decision was to perform an emergency Caesarean section immediately. That operation saved both my life and my daughter’s life. Although my body healed, my heart did not. Later, I overheard painful comments suggesting that I had been “too lazy to push.” Those words ignored the reality that I had spent fourteen hours fighting for my baby before reaching the point where my life itself was slipping away. Every time I looked at my Caesarean scar, those words echoed in my mind, reopening wounds that no one else could see. Years later, during my second pregnancy, I faced another life-changing challenge. I was diagnosed with placenta previa and experienced repeated bleeding throughout my pregnancy. Near the end of my seventh month, my waters began leaking continuously. My doctor instructed me to go to the hospital immediately, where it was determined that my baby could no longer remain safely in the womb because there was insufficient amniotic fluid. A second emergency Caesarean section was performed. My baby survived but required four days of specialized care in the Neonatal Intensive Care Unit. Like many families, we had planned financially for a normal delivery. Instead, the unexpected emergency surgery and NICU admission increased our hospital bill by more than 200 percent. Months later, I heard people suggest that I had been irresponsible with money and had unnecessarily chosen an expensive hospital. Once again, I found myself carrying emotional pain caused not by the surgery itself, but by judgment from people who had never experienced the reality of a medical emergency. Those two experiences changed my life forever. They taught me that many Caesarean mothers carry invisible scars that extend far beyond the operating theatre. They battle emotional trauma, misunderstanding, stigma, financial hardship, and silent grief while trying to care for the children whose lives they fought so hard to protect. That is why I founded MED C-SECTION WARRIORS NETWORK. Our mission is to ensure that no Caesarean mother walks this journey alone. We exist to provide support, education, advocacy, hope, and healing while changing the way society understands and responds to Caesarean birth. Every scar tells a story. Every story deserves to be heard. Every mother deserves dignity, compassion, and respect.
